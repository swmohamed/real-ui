# Anti-Patterns: The Generic AI Aesthetic (the forbidden-by-default list)

These patterns mark work as machine-generated. Each is banned by default;
allowed only with written contextual justification.

## The banned combo

1. **Purple→blue gradient hero** ("Stripe cosplay without Stripe") —
   Stripe's gradients are a brand system with 10 years of equity;
   imitating them signals imitation, not identity
2. **Glassmorphism cards everywhere** — translucent blur cards on
   gradient backgrounds: unreadable at 30% opacity over busy art,
   GPU-expensive in lists, and now the single strongest AI-generation tell
3. **Bento grids on everything** — Apple-marketing-derived tile mosaics
   pasted onto SaaS/banking/portfolios where content doesn't fit tiles
4. **Giant centered hero + tiny logo + "Trusted by" strip** formula —
   the default AI landing page skeleton
5. **Glowing borders / neon outline cards** — border glow + gradient
   border tricks on dark canvas
6. **Floating 3D blobs/random 3D objects** — decorative Spline-era
   shapes with no brand meaning, floating behind text
7. **Excessive radius inflation** — 24px+ radii on everything (inputs,
   text blocks, nav bars), pill-ification of dense UIs
8. **Gradient text headlines** (purple-cyan) as identity
9. **Stock "team high-five" + abstract tech lines** imagery era
10. **Feature-card walls of 6–9 identical icon tiles** with copy-generated
    titles ("Seamless Integration", "Powerful Analytics")
11. **Floating animation blobs** (CSS blur circles drifting) behind heroes
12. **Overuse of ↗ arrow links** as decorative pattern
13. **Centered-everything layouts** — every section center-aligned
    regardless of content shape
14. **Sparkle ✨ emoji / AI-star iconography** in marketing copy
15. **Dark mode + purple + glow as "premium tech" default**

## Why these fail (the reasoning, not just the rule)

- **They're context-free**: no industry, audience, region, or content
  logic produces them — that's why they read as machine output. Real
  banks are precise; real news is typographic; real luxury is restrained;
  none of them glow.
- **They fail functional tests**: contrast over gradients, readability
  through glass, hierarchy under uniform radius, performance under blur.
- **They're indistinguishable**: if the client can't tell their site from
  a competitor's generated one, the design added zero value.

## The justification bar (when a banned pattern may pass)

A banned pattern is usable ONLY when you can write this sentence with a
straight face: "This pattern is justified because [industry/audience/
brand/reference evidence], and here is the real-world reference class
that uses it well." Example passes: poster-scrim blur on a streaming
service (genre law); bento grid on an actual product-feature page with
real heterogeneous modules (Apple lineage); pill buttons in a kids'
game portal (register).

## Positive alternatives (what to do instead)

- Derive identity from INDUSTRY DNA (see visual-dna/) + content shape +
  one deliberate typographic decision
- Use the evidence: 4–8px radius product norm, one accent, real product
  artifacts, sector conventions
- Earn distinctiveness through typography scale, grid rhythm, and
  content hierarchy — the things real design leadership actually uses
- When in doubt: less decoration, more evidence (screenshots, numbers,
  specifics)
