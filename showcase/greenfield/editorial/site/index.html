Investigation 04 · Urban heat

The long block

Why one kilometer of road can be eight degrees hotter than the street beside it—and what the city knew before it was built.

Reported over six months · Data through July 2026 · 18 minute read

01 · The measurement02 · The permits03 · The shade gapEvidence index

At 2:17 in the afternoon, the thermometer on Block 14 read 49.2°C. Across the tramline, beneath a row of thirty-year-old trees, the same instrument read 41.1°C.

The difference was not a fluke. Groundwork placed calibrated sensors at twelve points and repeated the route on fourteen clear days. The hot block was consistently the outlier.

A decision hidden in the permits

Planning records show that the original shade requirement disappeared between the preliminary drawings and the construction issue. The reason appears only in a margin note: “utility clearance.” No replacement canopy was specified.

The shade gap is a service gap

People do not experience the street as a plan drawing. They experience it while waiting for a bus, pushing a stroller, or walking home from the clinic. The missing canopy changes who can use the block and when.
